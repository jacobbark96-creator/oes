With energy prices fluctuating and the push toward Net Zero intensifying, more UK homeowners than ever are asking the same question: **Can I get a grant for solar panels?**

The short answer is yes, but the landscape of government funding is complex and constantly evolving. In this guide, we break down exactly what funding is available in 2026, who qualifies, and how you can apply.

## 1. The Energy Company Obligation (ECO4)

The ECO4 scheme remains one of the primary avenues for securing fully funded or heavily subsidized solar panels. Designed to help low-income and vulnerable households improve their Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating, ECO4 places a legal obligation on medium and large energy suppliers to fund energy efficiency measures.

### Who is eligible for ECO4?
To qualify for ECO4 funding, you generally need to meet two criteria:
- **Income/Benefits:** You or someone in your household must receive qualifying benefits (such as Universal Credit, Pension Guarantee Credit, or Income-based JSA).
- **Property Inefficiency:** Your home must currently have an EPC rating of D, E, F, or G.

*Note: Even if you don't receive benefits, you may still qualify under the LA Flex (Local Authority Flexibility) route, which allows local councils to nominate households based on low income and vulnerability.*

## 2. The Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS)

While primarily focused on insulation (as the name suggests), GBIS often works in tandem with other upgrades. If your property requires significant fabric improvements (like solid wall or loft insulation) to bring it up to standard, securing this funding can free up capital for solar investments, or in some holistic retrofit scenarios, be bundled with renewable generation.

## 3. 0% VAT on Solar Installations

Even if you do not qualify for direct government grants, the UK government has extended the **0% VAT rate on the installation of energy-saving materials** (including solar panels and battery storage) until March 2027.

This effectively represents a 20% discount on the total cost of your system compared to standard consumer purchases.

## 4. Smart Export Guarantee (SEG)

While not an upfront grant, the SEG is crucial for the financial viability of solar panels. It requires large energy suppliers to pay you for the renewable electricity you export back to the National Grid.

By shopping around, you can secure rates that significantly shorten the payback period of your solar investment.
